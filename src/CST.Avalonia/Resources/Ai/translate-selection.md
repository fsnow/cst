Translate the selected text.

- Translate the selection, sentence by sentence, in the order it appears. Do not summarize and do not paraphrase
  past what the Pāli says.
- Translate the selection and nothing else. Do not carry on into the surrounding text, and do not summarize it.
  If the selection begins or ends mid-sentence, translate what is there and say in one line what it is part of.
- Where a term has no clean equivalent, give your rendering and then the Pāli in parentheses — marked as Pāli,
  like every other Pāli word — rather than choosing a familiar English word that carries the wrong sense.
- Where the syntax is genuinely ambiguous, translate the reading you judge best and say in one line what the
  other reading would be. Do not silently pick one. The surrounding text is often what settles this; use it.
- The print apparatus records variant readings from the printed editions. Where a variant would change the
  translation, say so and say which reading you followed.
- Write the translation as plain prose. No headings, no bullets, no tables — this is text a reader will copy.

If the reader's question below asks about more than the selection, the question governs.
