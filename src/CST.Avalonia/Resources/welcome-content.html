CST Reader

A Pāli Text Reader for the Chaṭṭha Saṅgāyana Tipiṭaka

Version 5.0.0-beta.6 • August 2026

Upgrading from Beta 5? Nothing to do.

Install over the top. Your settings, layout, open books and downloaded dictionaries carry across.

Coming from Beta 4 or earlier: delete your CST Reader data directory first — the search index changed in Beta 5 and an older one will not be read correctly.

macOS: ~/Library/Application Support/CSTReader/  •  Windows: %APPDATA%\CSTReader\

Focus Areas

Beta 5 reached feature parity with CST4. Beta 6 adds a great deal on top of it, and these areas are the newest — feedback on them is especially valuable:

The AI Assistant — brand new
  • Opens as a fourth tab in the left tool dock, beside Search, Open a Book and Dictionary
  • Select a passage and ask for a translation, a word-by-word breakdown, or an explanation
  • Turn it on under Settings › AI — then add a provider you have an account with under Settings › AI › Providers, a tab that appears once it is on
  • Your API key goes to the macOS Keychain or, on Windows, DPAPI under your user account — never into the app's settings file
  • If a key is already in your environment, the Providers tab offers it; nothing connects until you say so
  • Every answer shows what was actually sent to the model — is it the passage you meant?

This is the least-tested part of the release. Anything that looks wrong is worth reporting.

Access for AI clients — first release with testers
  • Off by default. Turn it on under Settings › AI › Access for AI Clients, which carries what a client needs
  • Does your agent get from there to reading the corpus without further help?
Windows on ARM — brand new
  • A native arm64 build, alongside the existing x64 one
  • Install and first launch: does the text download and index build complete?
  • If book text appears blank, turn off “Use hardware acceleration” under Settings › Configuration › Graphics, and restart
Reading
  • Find in Page — ⌘F / Ctrl+F searches within the book you are reading
  • Text zoom — ⌘/Ctrl with +, − and 0, or the percentage control in the book toolbar
  • Book fonts — choose a face for each script under Settings › Pali Script Fonts
  • Does your reading position survive zooming, switching tabs and restarting?
Windows and tabs
  • Float a book by dragging its tab out of the main window, and drag it back to re-dock
  • Split the reading area, and move a book between splits
  • Books that were floated or moved should all reopen when you restart

🚀 Quick Start

  • Open books: In the Open a Book tab, double-click a text — or select it and press Enter
  • Change script: Use the dropdown at the top (applies to all open books)
  • Search: Open the Search tab in the left panel (or View › Search), enter Pāli text, and optionally filter by book categories
  • Dictionary: Select a word and press ⌘D / Ctrl+D
  • Settings: Choose fonts for each script, and which dictionaries to use

Known Limitations

  • The texts are presented in Pāli only — CST Reader does not include translations. The built-in dictionaries give the meaning of individual words.
  • Menus and dialogs are English only. CST4 offers 24 interface languages; that work is still ahead.
  • macOS: idle CPU is higher than it should be, from a framework-level timer amplified by the text view. Beta 6 reduces how often that timer fires; the underlying issue is not ours to fix and is not yet closed
  • The AI Assistant can read a selection of roughly 1,100 characters in Devanāgarī, Myanmar and other non-Latin scripts, or about 2,900 in Latin. Beyond that the answer covers the surrounding passage instead, and says so

About the Chaṭṭha Saṅgāyana Tipiṭaka

The texts in this application come from the Sixth Council (Chaṭṭha Saṅgāyana) held in Myanmar from 1954-1956. This council, commemorating 2,500 years since the Buddha's Parinibbāna, produced the most authoritative edition of the Pāli Canon through careful comparison of manuscripts from Myanmar, Sri Lanka, Thailand, and Cambodia.

The texts have been carefully digitized and made available by the Vipassana Research Institute. We are deeply grateful to the scholars, editors, and meditators who participated in this monumental undertaking of preserving and sharing these teachings.

CST Reader provides digital access to all 217 texts from this edition, maintaining the authentic Pāli while offering modern tools for study and research.
